QR browser navigation vs beacons and apps

A side-by-side view of visitor friction, hardware, maintenance, and update speed — so facilities teams can choose deliberately.

Indoor navigation vendors often bundle three different ideas: a smartphone app, Bluetooth beacons (or similar radio hardware), and digital maps. Indian hospitals and campuses care about outcomes — fewer lost visitors, less desk interruption — not about radio protocols. This guide separates the options so you can ask better questions in demos.

What each approach actually requires

Native mobile apps

Visitors download an app, grant permissions, and learn a UI they may use once. For elective international airports that may be acceptable. For a parent bringing a child to OPD, download friction is high. App stores, OS versions, storage, and trust (“why does this hospital need an app?”) all become barriers. Apps can be powerful for staff workflows; they are a weak default for occasional visitors.

Beacon / hardware-assisted positioning

Beacons (and related indoor positioning hardware) help a device estimate location inside a building. Done well, they can support continuous positioning. Done poorly, they create a maintenance estate: batteries, mounting, RF interference, and recalibration when walls change. Procurement must include lifecycle cost, not only installation day cost. Security and biomedical teams may also have questions about devices placed throughout clinical areas.

QR codes opening browser routes

QR codes at known points (gate, lift lobby, desk) open a browser experience that already knows a starting landmark. The visitor chooses a destination and follows turn-by-turn guidance. Positioning is landmark- and instruction-based rather than continuous radio tracking. The trade-off is clear: you do not get free-roam “blue dot” everywhere, but you avoid app installs and beacon fleets. BootWay’s hospital navigation is built on this model — no app, no beacons.

Comparison table

Factor App + beacons QR + browser routes
Visitor start Install / update app, permissions Scan QR, opens in browser
Hardware in corridors Beacons or similar devices Printed or displayed QR codes
Maintenance Batteries, RF, firmware, app releases Map updates + QR placement checks
Layout change Map + possible beacon replan Update digital routes; reprint QR only if locations move
Language support Depends on app build Deliverable in visitor languages in the web UI
Best fit Staff tools, continuous tracking needs Patient/visitor wayfinding at scale

Questions to ask every vendor

  • Who updates the map when an OPD moves, and how long does it take?
  • What does year-two hardware maintenance cost?
  • Does the visitor need an account?
  • How are accessible (step-free) routes selected?
  • What offline or low-connectivity behaviour exists?
  • Who owns the map data if the contract ends?

When beacons still make sense

If you need continuous indoor positioning for asset tracking, staff workflows, or analytics that depend on fine-grained location streams, beacon or alternative IPS hardware may be justified. That is a different project from “help attendants find Lab 2.” Mixing the two goals in one RFP often produces an expensive system that patients still will not download.

A practical recommendation for Indian visitor wayfinding

Keep physical signage for orientation. Use QR-opened browser navigation for turn-by-turn help at high-friction journeys. Reserve apps and beacons for cases that truly need them.
